2 — What the bench codes mean

Three bench generations. Scores never compare across versions of the same task — a seat is decided on ONE bench, cited by code.

  • O — orchestration (fixture SYNC1): produce a grounded plan. Verdict PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL. Seeded trap: a red test baseline only visible if the model executes the tests.
  • Non-decision benches (archives only): I-v1 (impl PASS/FAIL) and DR-v1 (design review /10) — no seat is decided on them.
    • >
  • I-v2 — impl, bench v3 (fixture RG3): implement a feature + fix 4 seeded defects with regression tests. Score /14. Gates shelved as discriminator (pair-floor), scores still valid.
  • D — design authoring, bench v3 (substrate D1 + snaputil.py): write a design; 4 execution-revealed hazards seeded in the substrate. Score /11.
  • IR — impl review (SYNC1): review a commit with 3 seeded defects. Score n/3 + bonus − FPs.
  • DR-v2 — design review, bench v2 (fixture SYNC2): deeper critique, 10 categories + execution proofs. Score /14.

3 — What these benches measure — read before comparing to vendor benchmarks

What is measured: our seats do targeted implementation and review work on small codebases, and the benches score exactly that job — implement the feature (saturated: every frontier model does it), find the 4-3-10 seeded defects, prove the findings by executing, never claim a false contract. The score is dominated by the verification half, because the generation half no longer separates models.

Why the big expensive models rank below cheap ones HERE: their edge is long-horizon generative work — hours, thousands of tool calls. Our fixtures ask 30–60 minutes of easy generation plus rigor. The traps (a red test baseline; a comment claiming “this helper protects you”) catch overconfident claiming, which is the trained-in weakness of the big models. A Fable 5.5-class model at $4/task scores O 1/8 and D 2/11 on these benches while being a top generative model elsewhere — both facts are true; they measure different things.

Why not measure the long-horizon slice where the big models excel? Those runs are long and expensive — thousands of tool calls, hundreds of dollars per run for one data point. These benches sample the slice our seats actually do, at the volume we actually run it; measuring the rest is neither affordable nor decision-relevant.

What our numbers do NOT tell you: nothing about a model’s ability to do hard architectural work on large codebases — the I-v2 fixture is too small to sample that axis.

Scoring honesty: scores were first single-scorer (claude-haiku), then re-scored by a 4-family panel (self-family excluded) — the panel means are official; cells where scorers disagree by ≥2 pts carry a divergence flag. The bias concern is real and documented: the first pass over-scored its own family’s plans.

Method in brief

Each seat is decided on ONE bench, cited in the roster. Scores never compare across bench generations. Two-run arms are means; single-valid-run cells are starred (*) and provisional. Cost per task includes delegated child sessions. Official scores are 4-family panel means (the generating model's own family never scores it).
